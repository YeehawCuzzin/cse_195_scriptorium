




































Structure Matters: Evaluating Document Parsing Strategies for
LLM-Based Retrieval Systems

Krish Sarkar
University of California, Merced
krishsarkar@ucmerced.edu

Abstract

Document parsing is a critical but often over-
looked component of LLM-based retrieval sys-
tems. While most prior work focuses on im-
proving embedding models and retrieval strate-
gies, the quality of upstream document inges-
tion can significantly influence downstream per-
formance. In this work, we systematically eval-
uate multiple PDF parsing approaches, rang-
ing from flat text extraction to structure-aware
methods, to understand how parsing quality af-
fects document representation.

We implement a modular ingestion pipeline
and compare parsers including PyMuPDF, pdf-
plumber, PyPDF, Apache Tika, Unstructured,
Docling, and MinerU. Our analysis reveals that
parsing quality exists on a spectrum: flat ex-
traction methods introduce noise and lose struc-
tural information, while structure-aware parsers
preserve hierarchical organization and seman-
tic boundaries. Furthermore, advanced parsers
such as Docling and MinerU retain structured
artifacts such as Markdown headings, table
markup, image references, and extracted fig-
ures, enabling multimodal-ready document rep-
resentations.

Our findings demonstrate that higher-fidelity
parsing produces cleaner, more coherent doc-
ument segments and better preserves informa-
tion necessary for downstream processing. In
the retrieval evaluation, PyMuPDF achieved the
highest average cosine similarity, but Docling
produced the highest human-rated coherence,
showing that similarity scores alone do not fully
capture retrieval usefulness. We conclude that
investing in structure-aware parsing is a practi-
cal and impactful way to improve LLM-based
systems, even without modifying underlying
models.

1 Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have become
central to modern NLP systems (Vaswani et al.,

2017; Devlin et al., 2019), particularly in retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG) pipelines where ex-
ternal documents are incorporated to improve fac-
tual accuracy and contextual relevance. In such sys-
tems, unstructured data sources such as PDFs are
commonly ingested, processed, and transformed
into vector representations for semantic search.
The project follows the CSE 188 emphasis on LLM-
related work, experimental rigor, appropriate met-
ric choice, error analysis, and ACL-style commu-
nication (Wang, 2026). These criteria motivate the
paper’s focus on controlled parser comparison and
retrieval-oriented evaluation. More broadly, this
work can be viewed as a focused context engineer-
ing study: it examines how different preprocessing
choices change the information payload available
to a downstream retrieval system (Mei et al., 2025).

A critical step in these pipelines is document
parsing, where raw PDF files are converted into
representations suitable for downstream processing.
However, PDFs are inherently complex, containing
rich structural information such as section hierar-
chies, multi-column layouts, tables, figures, equa-
tions, and metadata. Document parsing research
emphasizes that parsing should preserve structural
relationships, semantic organization, and machine-
readable representations such as Markdown, JSON,
or XML rather than merely transcribing text (Zhang
et al., 2024). Many commonly used parsing ap-
proaches, such as flat text extraction, discard this
structural information, resulting in noisy or frag-
mented text. These artifacts propagate through
subsequent stages, affecting chunking quality, em-
bedding representations, and retrieved context.

In this work, we investigate how different docu-
ment parsing strategies impact downstream repre-
sentation quality in LLM-based retrieval systems.
We focus on the ingestion pipeline as a first-class
component of the system, rather than treating it
as a trivial preprocessing step. Specifically, we
compare a range of parsing approaches, including



flat extractors such as PyMuPDF, pdfplumber, and
PyPDF; semi-structured extraction through Apache
Tika; and structure-aware methods such as Unstruc-
tured, Docling, and MinerU. We analyze their ef-
fects on document representation, chunk coherence,
retrieval usefulness, and multimodal readiness.

Through empirical and qualitative analysis, we
show that structure-aware parsing improves the co-
herence and semantic integrity of document chunks.
In a small retrieval-based comparison, we find
that cosine similarity alone does not fully cap-
ture retrieval quality: some flat extraction methods
achieve competitive similarity scores while produc-
ing less coherent retrieved chunks. We further show
that these benefits come with tradeoffs in system
complexity, dependency management, and runtime
performance.

The contributions of this paper are as follows: (1)
we provide a systematic comparison of document
parsing strategies in the context of LLM-based re-
trieval pipelines; (2) we evaluate how parsing qual-
ity affects chunk coherence and rank-1 retrieval
usefulness across representative queries; and (3)
we highlight the importance of structure-aware in-
gestion as a key design consideration for building
reliable and effective LLM systems.

2 Related Work

2.1 Document Parsing and Structured
Extraction

Document parsing is the process of transforming
unstructured or semi-structured documents into
structured, machine-readable representations for
downstream applications such as knowledge base
construction and retrieval-augmented generation
(Zhang et al., 2024). Unlike traditional OCR, which
primarily focuses on transcribing text, document
parsing aims to preserve structural relationships,
semantic organization, reading order, and heteroge-
neous document elements such as tables, formulas,
figures, and charts (Zhang et al., 2024).

Recent document parsing research distinguishes
between pipeline-based systems and end-to-end
vision-language approaches. Pipeline-based sys-
tems decompose parsing into stages such as lay-
out detection, OCR, table recognition, formula
recognition, and reading-order recovery, while end-
to-end systems attempt to directly produce struc-
tured outputs such as Markdown, HTML, or LaTeX
(Ouyang et al., 2024). This distinction is impor-
tant for LLM-based retrieval because each parser

representation affects the form of the text and meta-
data that later become chunks, embeddings, and
retrieved context.

Modern benchmarks further show that document
parsing remains an open evaluation problem. Om-
niDocBench argues that document content extrac-
tion supports the data needs of LLMs and RAG
systems, but that existing benchmarks often have
limited document coverage, simplified evaluation
procedures, and insufficient fine-grained analysis
across content types (Ouyang et al., 2024). This
motivates evaluating parsers not only by whether
they extract text, but also by whether they preserve
useful structure for downstream systems. Recent
work on multimodal document understanding fur-
ther supports the importance of structure-aware in-
put representations. Liu et al. show that raw OCR
text can sometimes impair multimodal LLM doc-
ument understanding because it discards structure
and produces scattered attention, while structured
representations based on a LATEX-like format help
preserve hierarchy and spatial relationships (Liu
et al., 2025). This finding aligns with the motiva-
tion of our work: parser outputs should be evalu-
ated not only by text coverage, but also by how well
they preserve structure for downstream retrieval.

2.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation and
Chunking

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems
extend language models by retrieving relevant ex-
ternal documents or chunks at inference time. A
typical RAG pipeline includes ingestion, chunk-
ing, embedding, retrieval, and generation. In this
setting, retrieval quality depends not only on the
embedding model or similarity function, but also
on the quality of the document representation pro-
duced before embedding.

Chunking is a key design choice in RAG
pipelines. Prior RAG guidance emphasizes that
small chunks may focus retrieval but risk frag-
menting context, while large chunks preserve more
context but may introduce noise (Chiang, 2024).
Boundary selection is also important: headings,
paragraphs, lists, and tables often represent mean-
ingful semantic boundaries, so splitting purely by
character count can produce chunks that begin or
end in the middle of a coherent document unit (Chi-
ang, 2024). These concerns connect directly to
document parsing because a structure-aware parser
can expose headings, sections, tables, and figure
references that a chunking strategy can use.



Course project guidance from the Scripto-
rium/RefAIned ecosystem similarly emphasized
that document ingestion and preprocessing lay
the foundation for accurate downstream genera-
tion. In particular, the guidance highlighted auto-
mated loaders, chunking with overlap, metadata
tagging, and human review as mechanisms for im-
proving retrieval relevance, traceability, and de-
bugging (Pisman, 2025). Retrieval quality is also
sensitive to how information is represented before
retrieval. Zhu et al. identify a “lost-in-retrieval”
problem in multi-hop QA, where missing key enti-
ties degrade retrieval, disrupt the reasoning chain,
and lead to incorrect answers (Zhu et al., 2025).
Although our setting is single-document retrieval
rather than multi-hop QA, the same principle ap-
plies: upstream representation errors can prevent
the retriever from surfacing useful context.

2.3 Tables, Figures, and Multimodal
Document Elements

A major limitation of flat text extraction is that
it often collapses tables, figures, and other visual
elements into noisy text. This is especially prob-
lematic for academic papers, where tables and fig-
ures often contain central experimental evidence.
Structure-aware tools increasingly support the ex-
traction of non-textual elements as explicit artifacts.
For example, Unstructured documents support for
extracting embedded images and tables from PDFs
as Base64-encoded elements that can be decoded
and viewed separately (Unstructured, 2026).

Recent document parsing systems also empha-
size multimodal and layout-aware representations.
Uni-Parser, for example, describes scientific PDF
parsing as a process of preserving fine-grained
alignments across text, equations, tables, figures,
and chemical structures, with outputs such as
JSON, Markdown, and HTML intended for down-
stream LLM applications (Fang et al., 2025). While
this paper does not build a full multimodal RAG
system, the parser comparison shows why preserv-
ing visual and structural artifacts matters for future
retrieval pipelines.

2.4 Gap and Contribution
Prior work has separately studied document pars-
ing, RAG pipelines, chunking, and retrieval eval-
uation. However, the interaction between parsing
quality and retrieval usefulness remains underexam-
ined in practical student-built LLM systems. Many
pipelines still treat parsing as a solved preprocess-

ing step and begin evaluation only after text has
already been extracted.

This work addresses that gap by comparing mul-
tiple PDF parsing strategies inside a controlled in-
gestion and retrieval pipeline. By holding chunk-
ing, embedding, and retrieval settings constant
while varying parser output, we evaluate how ex-
traction artifacts and structural preservation affect
chunk coherence, rank-1 retrieval usefulness, and
multimodal readiness.

3 Methodology

We design a document ingestion pipeline that trans-
forms PDFs into structured representations suit-
able for downstream LLM-based retrieval systems.
Unlike traditional pipelines that rely on flat text
extraction, our approach explicitly evaluates multi-
ple parsing strategies to understand how structural
fidelity affects downstream processing.

Figure 1: Overview of the document ingestion and re-
trieval evaluation pipeline.

3.1 Pipeline Overview
As shown in Figure 1, the pipeline consists of
four stages: parsing, chunking, embedding, and
retrieval. In this work, we focus primarily on the
parsing stage, isolating its impact by holding the
downstream chunking, embedding, and retrieval
settings constant.

3.2 Parser Implementations
We evaluate multiple parsing approaches under a
unified interface:

• Flat text parsers: PyMuPDF, PyPDF, pdf-
plumber

• Semi-structured parsers: Apache Tika

• Structure-aware parsers: Unstructured, Do-
cling, MinerU, and related modern document
parsing systems (Unstructured, 2026; Fang
et al., 2025)

Each parser processes the same input document
and produces outputs that vary in structural fi-
delity and semantic organization. In addition to the



parsers used in the final retrieval comparison, the
project explored several additional tools, including
GROBID, Marker, and Nougat. These tools were
useful for evaluating differences in installation com-
plexity, output format, structural fidelity, and in-
tegration difficulty. However, because some tools
required separate environments, command-line run-
times, or external services, the final retrieval com-
parison focuses on the parsers with complete saved
outputs.

3.3 Structured Representations

Structure-aware parsers produce outputs that go
beyond plain text. Docling and MinerU generate
representations that include:

• Hierarchical text such as titles, sections, and
paragraphs

• Markdown structure such as headings, links,
and table markup

• Layout-aware information such as figures, ta-
bles, and image references

• Extracted visual elements, including tables
and figures as separate artifacts

These representations enable downstream sys-
tems to operate on semantically meaningful units
rather than arbitrary text spans.

3.4 Evaluation Criteria

Motivated by recent work arguing for comprehen-
sive document parsing evaluation across layout
categories, content types, and document attributes
(Ouyang et al., 2024), we evaluate parsing outputs
along four dimensions:

• Structural Fidelity: Preservation of docu-
ment hierarchy

• Readability: Cleanliness and coherence of
extracted text

• Layout Fidelity: Preservation of tables, fig-
ures, and formatting

• Processing Efficiency: Runtime and com-
plexity

This evaluation allows us to analyze how parsing
quality influences the overall effectiveness of LLM-
based pipelines.

4 Experiments

We design our experiments to evaluate how differ-
ent document parsing strategies affect downstream
processing in an LLM-based retrieval pipeline. By
holding all components of the pipeline constant
except for the parsing stage, we isolate the impact
of parsing quality on chunking, embedding, and
retrieval.

4.1 Dataset
We conduct the final retrieval experiment on an
IEEE-style academic PDF, the SLAM-LLM paper,
which contains multi-column formatting, section
hierarchies, tables, figures, and dense technical
content. This document provides a realistic test
case for evaluating parsing robustness and struc-
tural preservation.

During development, additional parser outputs
and tooling experiments were explored, but the
reported retrieval comparison uses this single rep-
resentative paper to keep the parser comparison
controlled and reproducible.

4.2 Parsing Methods Compared
We evaluate a range of commonly used PDF pars-
ing tools:

• PyMuPDF: A lightweight, fast parser that per-
forms flat text extraction with minimal struc-
tural awareness.

• pdfplumber: A text extraction tool with basic
layout handling but substantial sensitivity to
multi-column formatting.

• PyPDF: A general-purpose PDF parser that
extracts raw text but may introduce spacing
artifacts.

• Apache Tika: A document extraction frame-
work that produces readable text but may in-
clude blank-space and page-header artifacts.

• Unstructured: A structure-aware parsing
framework that identifies document elements
such as narrative text, titles, and list items.

• Docling: A structure-aware parser that pro-
duces Markdown output with headings, links,
image placeholders, and table markup.

• MinerU: A structured document processing
tool designed to preserve layout, semantic hi-
erarchy, and visual document elements.



These methods span a spectrum from flat extrac-
tion to structure-aware parsing, enabling compara-
tive analysis of their effects on downstream tasks.

4.3 Experimental Setup

For each parser, we process the same input PDF and
save the extracted output as plain text or Markdown,
depending on the parser. These outputs are then
passed through a consistent downstream pipeline:

• Chunking: Text is segmented into fixed-
size chunks of approximately 1200 characters
with 200-character overlap. Prior RAG guid-
ance emphasizes that chunk size, overlap, and
boundary selection affect whether retrieved
information remains useful or becomes frag-
mented (Chiang, 2024).

• Embedding: Each chunk is encoded using the
all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model, producing 384-
dimensional embeddings.

• Retrieval: Query embeddings are compared
against chunk embeddings using cosine sim-
ilarity. For each query-parser pair, the top-
ranked chunk is inspected and manually rated
for relevance and coherence.

This standardized setup holds chunking, embed-
ding, and retrieval settings constant so that ob-
served differences can be attributed primarily to
parser output quality. The evaluation uses five rep-
resentative queries about the SLAM-LLM paper
and compares rank-1 retrieval results across seven
parser outputs.

4.4 Qualitative Evaluation of Parsing

We first evaluate parsing outputs qualitatively by
examining:

• Structure preservation: whether section
headings and document hierarchy are main-
tained.

• Text integrity: whether words and sentences
are extracted without corruption or merging
artifacts.

• Noise levels: presence of headers, footers, or
irrelevant content.

These factors directly influence the quality of
chunked text and subsequent embeddings.

4.5 Chunking and Representation Analysis

We analyze how parsing affects chunk formation
by inspecting the resulting segments. In flat ex-
traction methods, chunk boundaries are often mis-
aligned with semantic units, leading to fragmented
or mixed-content chunks. In contrast, structure-
aware parsing produces chunks that better corre-
spond to logical sections of the document.

4.6 Retrieval Evaluation

To assess downstream impact, we perform a
retrieval-based evaluation using representative nat-
ural language queries. For each parsing method, we
retrieve the top-k most similar chunks and evaluate:

• Relevance: whether retrieved chunks contain
information relevant to the query.

• Coherence: whether chunks form complete
and interpretable units of meaning.

Example queries include questions targeting key
aspects of the document, such as definitions, con-
tributions, and system descriptions.

4.7 Implementation Details

All experiments are conducted using Python-based
tooling. Parsing is performed using the respective
libraries for each method, while embedding gener-
ation is handled through the SentenceTransformers
framework. The system is executed on a local envi-
ronment, and all methods are applied to identical
input documents to ensure consistency.

based on structural preservation, readability, layout

5.1 Text Quality and Structural Preservation

Flat extraction methods frequently produce noisy
outputs, including merged tokens, broken spac-
ing, and loss of document hierarchy. For exam-
ple, pdfplumber produced artifacts such as “JOUR-
NALOFLATEXCLASSFILES,VOL.14,NO.8” and
merged words, while PyPDF introduced spacing ar-
tifacts such as separated letters in headings. These
artifacts degrade readability and disrupt semantic
segmentation.

In contrast, structure-aware parsers preserve
more logical organization. Unstructured labels
elements such as titles, narrative text, and list
items, while Docling and MinerU preserve section-
like structure through Markdown headings, table
markup, image references, and extracted visual ele-
ments.

5.2 Table and Figure Preservation

A key distinction emerges in the handling of non-
textual elements. Flat parsers fail to preserve tab-
ular structure, often collapsing tables into unread-
able text. In contrast, modern structure-aware tools
increasingly support extracting images, tables, and
other layout-sensitive elements as explicit artifacts.
Unstructured, for example, provides documented
support for extracting embedded images and tables
from PDFs as Base64-encoded visual elements,
which can then be decoded and viewed separately
(Unstructured, 2026). In our experiments, MinerU
extracts tables and figures as separate image assets,
maintaining their visual and semantic integrity.

This capability allows structured parsers to retain
information that would otherwise be lost, particu-
larly in documents where tables and figures contain
critical data. Figure 2 shows examples of tables and
diagrams extracted by MinerU as separate visual
artifacts from the SLAM-LLM paper.

5.3 Structured Output Representations

Structure-aware parsers produce richer outputs than
plain text extraction. MinerU produces layout-
aware outputs that include bounding boxes, hi-
erarchical labels (e.g., title, paragraph), and seg-
mentation of document elements. Docling pro-
duces Markdown output with headings, links, im-
age placeholders, and table markup. These repre-
sentations enable downstream systems to perform
more precise chunking and semantic indexing.

6 Analysis

Our results reveal that document parsing exists on a
spectrum of structural fidelity, rather than a binary
distinction between correct and incorrect extrac-
tion.

6.1 Parsing Spectrum
We identify three levels of parsing quality:

• Flat Parsing: Produces raw text with min-
imal structure, often introducing noise and
fragmentation.

• Clean Text Parsing: Produces readable text
but lacks hierarchical or semantic organiza-
tion.

• Structured Parsing: Preserves document hi-
erarchy, layout, and semantic groupings.

This spectrum directly influences the quality of
downstream processing.

6.2 Propagation of Errors
Errors introduced during parsing propagate through
the pipeline. Poor parsing leads to incoherent

chunks, which in turn produce weaker or less inter-
pretable retrieved results. The retrieval comparison
supports this pattern: pdfplumber achieved a mod-
erate average similarity score but received the low-
est relevance and coherence ratings. Conversely,
Docling achieved the highest coherence score de-
spite not having the highest average similarity. This
indicates that retrieval usefulness depends not only
on vector similarity, but also on whether the re-
trieved chunk remains semantically coherent.

6.3 Multimodal-Ready Parsing

A key insight from our experiments is that ad-
vanced parsers such as Docling and MinerU pro-
duce outputs that are not limited to plain text. Do-
cling preserves Markdown structure, table markup,
links, and image placeholders, while MinerU addi-
tionally extracts tables and figures as separate arti-
facts. These parsers enable a form of multimodal-
ready parsing, where both textual and visual infor-
mation are preserved.

This expands the capabilities of document in-
gestion pipelines beyond traditional text-based pro-
cessing and opens opportunities for integrating mul-
timodal LLM systems.

6.4 Tradeoffs

While structure-aware parsers provide higher-
quality outputs, they introduce tradeoffs in com-
putational complexity, runtime, and dependency
management. Simpler parsers remain useful in sce-
narios where speed and simplicity are prioritized.
For rapid prototyping or lightweight applications,
flat extraction may be sufficient. For systems where
retrieval quality, traceability, or document fidelity
are important, structure-aware parsing is more ap-
propriate.

The results also align with design guidance from
the broader Scriptorium/RefAIned project context,
where document ingestion was treated as the foun-
dation for downstream retrieval and question gener-
ation. In that setting, preprocessing quality, chunk
overlap, and metadata tagging were identified as
important for relevance, traceability, and debugging
(Pisman, 2025). Our parser comparison provides
an empirical example of this principle: extraction
artifacts introduced before embedding can persist
into retrieval results and reduce human-rated coher-
ence.



6.5 Limitations and Future Work

A limitation of this evaluation is that the retrieval
study uses a small set of representative queries
over a single academic paper. The results should
therefore be interpreted as an exploratory compar-
ison rather than a large-scale benchmark. Future
work could extend the evaluation to more document
types, larger query sets, and automated relevance
judgments.

6.6 Summary

Overall, our findings demonstrate that parsing qual-
ity is a critical factor in LLM-based pipelines. In-
vesting in structure-aware parsing provides a prac-
tical pathway to improving system performance
without modifying core models.

7 Conclusion

In this work, we investigated the role of document
parsing in LLM-based retrieval systems and demon-
strated that it is a critical factor in determining
downstream performance. While prior work has
largely focused on model architectures and retrieval
strategies, our findings show that the quality of doc-
ument ingestion can have an equally significant
impact.

Through a controlled comparison of parsing
methods, we showed that flat text extraction ap-
proaches often fail to preserve document structure,
leading to degraded chunking and less coherent
retrieved results. In contrast, structure-aware pars-
ing methods retain semantic hierarchy and produce
more coherent document representations. Our re-
trieval comparison further shows that higher cosine
similarity does not necessarily correspond to better
human-rated relevance or coherence.

Our analysis highlights a clear relationship be-
tween parsing quality and downstream representa-
tion quality, emphasizing that errors introduced dur-
ing early stages of the pipeline propagate through
subsequent components. These results suggest that
improving upstream data processing is a practical
and impactful approach to enhancing LLM systems
without modifying core models.

Overall, this work underscores the importance of
treating document ingestion as a first-class design
consideration. By incorporating structure-aware
parsing into retrieval pipelines, developers can im-
prove the quality of retrieved context and build
more reliable, interpretable, and effective LLM-
based applications.
